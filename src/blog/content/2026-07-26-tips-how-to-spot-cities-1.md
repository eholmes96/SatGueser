When you are looking at a landscape that you don't recognize, or can't quite put a name to, try thinking through a few of these:

## Where's the Water?

One of the first things I look for are water features. Is there a river? A large body of water like an ocean or a lake? What direction does it face? Nearly all cities are based around water features. If the city has no water, that's a great clue (Johannesburg, Atlanta, Las Vegas, and Charlotte). Most cities are situated with the ocean to the East or West, and very few have large bodies of water to the North (Cleveland with Lake Erie, or Jakarta with the Java Sea) or to the south (Melbourne, Singapore, Cape Town, Istanbul, Toronto). Remember that big bodies of water aren't always oceans, they are often lakes, especially in the U.S. game.

Many cities are just situated on rivers or straits, these can often be a little more difficult to recognize immediately, but you can try to remember a few key characteristics: direction, straight/curviness, river width, and number of bridges. These can really help quickly identify cities.

## How's the Climate?

Most cities around the world tend to be built in pretty green areas, but sometimes us humans like to build cities where it just don't rain! Southwestern American cities like Phoenix, Las Vegas, SLC and El Paso all look pretty dry. Cairo has a pretty unique mix of desert and fertile, green farmland. Other common places for desert cities are in the Middle East (Dubai, Doha, Riyadh, Tehran, etc) as well as some less expected places like Lima, Peru, which sits on the west coast of South America. Johannesburg, Madrid, and Mexico City are all big inland cities with semi-dry looking surroundings (some green, some yellow/orange).

## Grid Design

What does the grid look like? U.S. Cities tend to have defined, square grids in the central core, with sprawling suburbs surrounding. They are also distinctive because we love our big wide highways with massive interchanges. European and older Middle Eastern cities tend to have more chaotic grids, typically radiating from a central point. Asian cities tend to look especially dense, and typically have mostly narrow streets and a lot of high-rises. But these hints can lead you astray at times so beware! Buenos Aires looks very European, while many non-U.S. cities have large highways cutting through the center of town.
